Why spell?

Why spell exists, and how it could change who gets to make software.

Status: in dialog · being talked through Updated: 2026-10-01 Notes for agents: motivation.md

1. Summary
  • Spell picks up HyperCard's promise for the web: people who don't call themselves programmers can read, change and make real software, in plain English.
  • Your 2017 talk says it best: a "non-programmer programming environment" where a layperson can write an app, or customize and mash up apps for their own use.
  • What's new since 2017: AI writes code now. That may make code a person can read and check worth more, not less (a draft thought: see Q3).
  • Everything below is pieced together from the talk and old notes. This page needs your words.

2. Today

Every lay person I've talked about "english as a programming language" to has said "Why doesn't it do that already???"

Your 2017 Future of Programming talk
  • The talk (packages/spell/thoughts/FOP presentation.txt), "Why English?":
    • people can READ programs, even before they can write one
    • "it's just English" invites experimentation
    • code documents itself: literate programming
    • talking interfaces are coming, and semicolons won't cut it
  • What spell fixes about HyperCard (the talk's slide): Mac only, no network, an impenetrable binary format, only seven kinds of data, and hard for a layperson to extend.
  • What is spell (packages/spell/thoughts/WhatIsSpell.md): drag-and-drop layout plus an English language; runs anywhere JavaScript runs; open source and "always free for personal use".
  • The lineage: HyperCard ("programming for the rest of us"), SuperCard, Oracle Media Objects (which you worked on), Inform, literate programming, Bret Victor.

4. Ideas
  1. I1 Readable is the new writable AI can write code; people still need to read, trust and change itdetails

    If an AI writes your app in spell, you can read what it did, check it, and fix the one line that's wrong. Readable source becomes the contract between a person and their software.

  2. I2 Climb at your own pace HyperCard's levels: browse, type, paint, author, scriptdetails

    Everyone starts by using a stack someone made. Some start changing it. A few write their own. Nobody is told they must learn to program first.

  3. I3 Software you own your stack, your data, on your machine, no subscription
  4. I4 Fix HyperCard's five limits web not Mac-only, networked, text files, rich types, a language people can extend

5. Questions
  1. Q1 Who is spell for, first? settled in audiencedetails

    Context: the talk says "layperson"; the notes never mention kids or teachers. See audience.

    Suggestion: pick one person to design for through December, and name the others as "later".

  2. Q2 What does "change the world" look like? a concrete picture, five years outdetails

    Suggestion: describe one ordinary week in 2031 for someone who uses spell. What did they make? Who did they share it with? What would they have done without it?

  3. Q3 Where does AI fit in the why? reaction, partner, or neither details

    Options:

    • partner: AI writes spell; people read and own it (I1)
    • teacher: AI helps people learn to write it themselves
    • neither: spell stands on its own; AI is a feature

    Suggestion: partner, with readable source as the point. Decides a lot in ai.

  4. Q4 Open source, and how it keeps going still "free for personal use"? SpellCo as a B-Corp?details

    Context: WhatIsSpell.md imagined SpellCo, a B-Corp, earning from hosting, a marketplace, training and support. The repo is MIT today.

    Suggestion: keep it MIT and leave money out of the three-month plan, but say in public that it's open source and will stay that way.

  5. Q5 Which HyperCard ideas are sacred? and which are not coming backdetails

    Candidates: stacks and cards; the levels in I2; everything is editable while it runs; the message path (button → card → background → stack); a built-in database; scripts that live on objects.

  6. Q6 The one-sentence pitch to react to, not to keepdetails

    Drafts:

    • "Spell is programming in plain English, for people who never wanted to learn to code."
    • "Make the software you wish existed, in words you already know."
    • "HyperCard, reborn for the web, in plain English."

6. Risks
  1. R1 English-like languages have a long graveyard COBOL, AppleScript: easy to read, hard to writedetails

    HyperTalk's critics called English-like code "write-only": it reads naturally, but guessing the exact words the computer accepts is hard. The why must say how spell is different: an editor that suggests, and asks when unsure.

  2. R2 "Just ask the AI" is the competitor people may skip code entirelydetails

    If an AI can make the app, why read code at all? The answer has to be convincing on the home page.

  3. R3 A vision too wide to ship language, editor, UI library, native, AI, translation — in three monthsdetails

    The why should help us say "not yet" to most of it.

7. Decisions
  1. D1 AI: partner, then teacher AI writes spell; reading what it wrote is how people learn to write their owndetails

    Why: AI writing the app answers R2 head-on: spell is how you check and change what it made (I1). Reading it is also the first rung of I2: people who read AI-written spell start fixing a line, then writing their own.
