ConsoleLines Console lines, groups and logged values: what every console draws its lines with.

The lines of a spell program's console, such as what its print statements say, drawn as a tree: a group of lines opens and closes under its own line. Every console in the app draws its lines with it, the editor's and each runner's.

Written for: anyone changing a console in the app, or reading its code Live: a real <spell-viewer>, showing its console

FizzBuzz, a program that only prints, so its runner shows its console where an app would be: every line in it is drawn by these components. The numbers are numbers, so they're coloured as numbers; the words are text.

Live, from the app's element build, which this set's pages load: /element/spell.pack.js. The app package builds it with yarn build:element.

A console hands it its lines, and keeps them live. The runners' console, under every <spell-viewer>, is little more than this:

RunnerConsole.tsx · a runner's console · 10 lines
  • ConsoleLines draws a list of lines: each one a ConsoleLine, or, for a group, a ConsoleGroup.
  • A group is its own line with an arrow, then its lines under it, one step further in: click the arrow to open or close it. The editor's console logs its compiling and running as groups.
  • Each value a line logs is a ConsoleObject, which picks how to show it: a number, some text, a date, "Array(3)" for a list. It draws it as a ConsoleValue, styled by its kind.
  • Click an object's value to log it to the browser's devtools as it. In the editor, a parsed piece of spell is selected in the code too.
  • lines is a plain list: the caller keeps it live, as above. A new line logged adds just its own row.
  • It needs nothing of the editor's or the parser's, so a runner can show a console without them. The editor's ConsoleViewer adds what it knows, such as parsed spell.
  • Its look assumes it's inside a box with the ConsoleViewer class, as above.
