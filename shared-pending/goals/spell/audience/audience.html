Who it's for

The people we imagine using spell, what they want to make, and how they'll find it.

Status: draft · first pass, not yet talked through Updated: 2026-10-01 Notes for agents: audience.md

1. Summary
  • The 2017 answer: laypeople: people who can read a program before they can write one. The test reader in old notes: "a high-school educated person".
  • What they'd make (WhatIsSpell.md): client lists, home budgets, family calendars, collections, invoices, dashboards, catalogs, games.
  • Not mentioned anywhere yet: kids and teachers. Programmers appear as the people who extend spell.
  • For December: we need ONE person to design for. The rest can wait their turn.

2. Today
  • Examples: FizzBuzz, Calculator, Todos, Solitaire. Fun, but they say "toy", not "tool".
  • No path in: no onboarding, tutorial, or docs written for people writing spells.
  • The app assumes a developer: a clone, yarn start, and parser debug panes on screen.

4. Ideas
  1. I1 The tinkerer makes small tools for themselves: a collection, a budget, a game for the kidsdetails

    HyperCard's heart. Wants it to feel like a craft, not a course.
  2. I2 The small-business owner customers, invoices, schedulesdetails

    Needs data, forms and printing: depends on thingverse.

  3. I3 The learner and their teacher a first language you can read alouddetails

    Needs a gentle path, great errors, and classroom-friendly devices (Chromebooks).
  4. I4 The programmer who wants readable code writes libraries, embeds spell as their app's scripting languagedetails

    See programmers.
  5. I5 The kid who outgrew Scratch games, stories, things to show friends
  6. I6 The spreadsheet power user analysts, scientists, office heroes who already "program" in formulas

5. Questions
  1. Q1 Who is the first person? one of I1-I6, or someone elsedetails

    Suggestion: the tinkerer (I1). It's who HyperCard served best, it fits a Mac app, and their projects (collections, budgets, games) need the least infrastructure. The small-business owner is a strong second, but needs data and printing first.

  2. Q2 Read first, or write first? is the first experience using a stack, or writing one?details

    Suggestion: read first. Open a finished example, use it, then "show me how it works" and change one line. That's HyperCard's browse-to-script ladder, and it plays to spell's strength: being readable.

  3. Q3 Where will they find spell? word of mouth, Hacker News, YouTube, teachers...
  4. Q4 Do people share what they make? in December, or later?details

    Sharing needs hosting or a link format; see web-components.

  5. Q5 What computer are they on? Mac? Windows? iPad? a school Chromebook?details

    Decides a lot in native.
  6. Q6 Who are your five testers? kinds of people, or names
