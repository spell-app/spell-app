AI

AI inside the spell app, and AI that helps people write spells.

Status: draft · first pass, not yet talked through Updated: 2026-10-01 Notes for agents: ai.md

1. Summary
  • No AI features yet.
  • Spell suits AI unusually well: the source is English, so the person who asked can read and check what an AI wrote.
  • Two jobs: AI in the app (explain, write, fix, translate), and AI building spell (agents already write most of the code).
  • The big question: is spell "the language AI writes for you, so you can read it"?

2. Today
  • In the app: nothing. The language server offers parser-driven completion and a "Define to <phrase>" quick fix.
  • Hooks we have: the parser's "expecting" mode knows what may come next; spell explain <word> (cli branch) shows a rule's syntax and an example.
  • Building spell: mostly Claude agents, guided by AGENTS.md, skills (.claude/skills/) and plan docs.

4. Ideas
  1. I1 The parser as guardrail every AI suggestion is parsed before it's shown; only valid spell appears
  2. I2 Generation guided by the parser the expecting mode lists what may come next: constrain the model with it
  3. I3 Spell tools for any agent an MCP server: compile, check, explain, rundetails

    The cli already has most of these as commands. See cli.

  4. I4 Bring your own key an API key in settings first; hosted or local models later
  5. I5 AI as a patient teacher explains errors kindly, suggests what to try next

5. Questions
  1. Q1 Which models, and who pays? Claude by default? pluggable? people's own keys?details

    Suggestion: Claude, with the person's own key, for the preview. No billing to build.

  2. Q2 AI in the December preview? or afterdetails

    Suggestion: one small, delightful feature ("explain this line"), or none.
  3. Q3 How much should AI do? whole apps, or a line at a time?details

    If AI writes whole apps, people may never learn to read them. If it only helps line by line, it's slower but teaches. Perhaps both, with "show me what you did" always on.

  4. Q4 Privacy people's spells sent to a model: opt-in, always visible
  5. Q5 Should AI pick between readings? or keep ambiguity deterministicdetails

    Suggestion: keep the parser deterministic. AI may suggest a rewrite, but never silently choose a meaning.

  6. Q6 Is spell hidden in "do it all"? or still there to read, just not pushed at peopledetails

    Context: motivation/I1 makes readable source the point; "do it all for me" may not want to see it.

    Suggestion: never hidden, just not in the way: one click to "show me the spell".

6. Risks
  1. R1 AI makes the language unnecessary people ask for apps and never look at codedetails

    The answer: readable source is the contract. See motivation/I1.

  2. R2 English that looks right but isn't spell models will invent phrases; the parser must check every one
  3. R3 Cost and keys hosted AI costs money; people's own keys add friction

7. Decisions
  1. D1 People pick how much AI teaches three modes: do it all for me, show and explain on ask, walk me through itdetails

    Your words: "do it all for me", plus both of:

    1. do it all for me: AI writes the whole app
    2. show, explain on ask: AI may write whole apps; its spell is always on screen; it explains any line when asked
    3. walk me through it: after each change, AI narrates what it wrote, line by line

    Why: the teacher half of motivation/D1, at the pace each person wants.
