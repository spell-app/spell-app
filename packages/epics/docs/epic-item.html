<epic-item> One item of an epic page: a question, judgement call, caveat, todo, issue or test, drawn as a line that unfolds.

One item of an epic page: a question, a judgement call, a caveat, a todo, an issue or a test, all ONE element, the kind told by its id's first letter. It draws the item's line (a fold arrow, the coloured id chip, the title, its review label and buttons), and folds its details away under it.

For anyone reading or writing an epic page's markup, or changing this element.

A slot is a place in the drawing that a child goes to: a child with slot="name" goes to that name's place, every other child to the default one (shown as default).

Slot What goes there
default Its details.
title A title with markup, in place of title.
status Claude's status cards (<epic-status slot="status">, P13): drawn last in its details, under Owen's marked note, above the note box.

A part is a piece of what the element draws in its shadow root (its own hidden markup), named so a stylesheet can reach it: epic-item::part(header).

Part What it is
base The item.
line Its line: fold button, id chip, title, review label, actions; sticky while open.
toggle The fold <button> (the chevron), on an item with details.
id The id chip (Q7), a link to the item, in its state's colour. While the page is reviewed, an open judgement call's or issue's chip is a button instead: urgent (red) <-> not urgent (yellow).
title The title.
review The review label: reviewed 10/6/26, deferred, to do. Not while the page is reviewed: the review buttons' tooltips say it then.
overnight With overnight: the bed icon at the line's end, made overnight.
git With commits (commits, or <epic-commit> children): a git icon at the line's end that shows or hides this item's own commits, whatever the page's git toggle says (T17); pressed while they show.
actions The box at the end of the line: the git icon, the review label, then the review buttons.
details Its details: hidden while folded.
label Original question / Original reply over its own text.
error With source: the line saying the part couldn't be loaded.
review-buttons The review buttons (P9): the note bubble, a pick's letter, Approve / Revisit / Make Todo, then Do Now. Only while the page is reviewed (served by the page server, its inbox answering).
note-box The note box: the note, then Revisit Later and Make Todo.
said A note marked and closed: You · revisit soon · sent 10:42, the note, Edit.

State When

loaded With source: the part is in its children.
error With source: the part couldn't be loaded.
commits Its own commits show: its git icon pressed.
Event Detail Cancelable When
ui-open { open: boolean, item: Element, originalEvent?: Event } yes About to unfold: its line was clicked; after the fact (not cancelable) for a link or find-in-page. Cancel to stay folded.

The colours mean the same on every doc page: the whole scheme is on Epic Page Colours (its source: plan-doc.md, "Colours").

The chip shows the id in capitals (Q7) and is a link to the item. Its colour is the item's state, solid:

state colour means
attention red needs Owen: an open question; an open judgement call or issue not reviewed yet
progress blue Claude is working on it (an underway status card, or working)
open yellow open, still undecided, or work a review queued; dark text on it
recent green decided or done, however long ago
old grey canceled: no longer relevant
  • A canceled item's title is struck through.
  • An item made overnight (overnight) has a small bed icon at the end of its line.
  • While Owen's review mark on it is live (pressed, or sent but not yet handled), the chip wears that button's colour and fill instead (see below). Once Claude has handled the mark, the chip goes back to its state's colour: the result.
  • While the page is reviewed, an open judgement call's or issue's chip is a button: a click flips it between urgent (red) and not urgent (yellow, calm).

While an epic page is being reviewed (<epic-page reviewing>, served by the page server), its marks go to the doc's review inbox: the file of Owen's marks that Claude reads (<name>.inbox.json).

Then the line ends with four buttons: Approve (green), Revisit (blue), Make Todo (green) in one group, and Do Now (blue, a paper plane) apart. Each wears the fill rule, which says how far a mark has got:

  • a grey outline: available
  • dashed in its colour: Owen pressed it, not sent yet
  • outlined in its colour: sent (or a Do Now taken, its icon turning while Claude works)
  • a Do Now with nobody listening stays dashed, "waiting for a session": the page server wakes the epic (types /epic review <name> into its session) and the tooltip says how
  • a grey outline again once Claude has handled it: the chip shows the result

Out of review, the line shows a review label instead: reviewed 10/6/26, deferred, or to do (yellow).

  • An item starts folded: only its line shows. A click on the line unfolds it, and so do a link to it (#q7, or an id listed in its part-ids) and find-in-page.
  • While open, its line sticks to the top as you scroll its details.
  • Its details: a question's text under a small Question label, then its text, option cards, the answer, More Details, replies, earlier versions and commits. An answered question's own text is labelled Original question; an item with More Details labels its text Original reply.
  • open is page state: the tool never writes it, so a doc always opens folded.

Only the epic tool (spell dev plan-doc) writes items, and it rewrites every item's state on every edit.

  • add <name> question|judgement|caveat|issue|todo|test|decision "title" [--details html] [--calm]: a new item, its id the next of its kind; a decision is a question born answered
  • decide, close, reopen, cancel: its status
  • review, defer, queue / unqueue, and the inbox's apply, working and done: its review marks (reviewed, deferred, queued, work, working, review-as, calm)
  • details <name> <id> --file html [--append | --more]: its details; a rewrite moves the old text into its Original Discussion (<epic-original>), never dropping it
  • status: Claude's status cards (<epic-status>); commit: its commits
  • split / join: its details into a part file (source, part-ids) and back
  • older docs: the one-time converter (plan-doc convert, convert/) made every item from the old markup
