## Context

- **The 2017 answer:**  laypeople:  people who can read a program before they can write one.  The test reader in
  old notes:  "a high-school educated person".
- **What they'd make** (`WhatIsSpell.md`):  client lists, home budgets, family calendars, collections, invoices,
  dashboards, catalogs, games.
- **Not mentioned anywhere yet:**  kids and teachers.  Programmers appear as the people who extend spell.
- **For December:**  we need ONE person to design for.  The rest can wait their turn.

### Today

- **Examples:**  FizzBuzz, Calculator, Todos, Solitaire.  Fun, but they say "toy", not "tool".
- **No path in:**  no onboarding, tutorial, or docs written for people writing spells.
- **The app assumes a developer:**  a clone, `yarn start`, and parser debug panes on screen.

## Open questions (ask, don't decide)

- **Q1 · Who is the first person?** -- one of I1-I6, or someone else
- **Q2 · Read first, or write first?** -- is the first experience using a stack, or writing one?
- **Q3 · Where will they find spell?** -- word of mouth, Hacker News, YouTube, teachers...
- **Q4 · Do people share what they make?** -- in December, or later?
- **Q5 · What computer are they on?** -- Mac?  Windows?  iPad?  a school Chromebook?
- **Q6 · Who are your five testers?** -- kinds of people, or names

## Goals (direction, not orders)

- **Now → December 2026:**
  - **G1 · One person to design for** -- chosen, named and written up as a short story
  - **G2 · Five real testers** -- people you know who'll try the December preview and tell the truth
  - **G3 · Their first hour** -- the app that person makes in their first hour:  it becomes an example and a guide
- **2027:**
  - **G4 · The second and third people** -- who comes next, in order
  - **G5 · A place to gather** -- a forum or chat where people help each other
- **Someday:**
  - **G6 · Clubs and classrooms** -- if teachers turn out to be the people who need it

## Risks to keep in mind

- **R1 · Designing for everyone** -- means designing for no one
- **R2 · Examples that say "toy"** -- Solitaire is delightful, but signals games, not tools
